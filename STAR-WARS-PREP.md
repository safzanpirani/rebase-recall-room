# Star Wars weekly prep

Open `index.html` and choose **Star Wars prep · 256 cards**. Take an Adaptive 12 before reading the reference below. Use the result to find your gaps.

The four decks contain 64 questions each. They cover the nine-film saga, Rogue One, Solo, selected television series, and some games and publishing history. The theme announcement gives no narrower scope. The questions are practice material based on public sources.

## Four study sessions

Allow roughly 25–35 minutes per session. If you know little Star Wars, read the film map first and allow extra time for explanations.

1. **Foundations, Set 7.** Finish one Full deck. Read each answer note. Learn identities, family relationships, planets, and film order.
2. **Film details, Set 8.** Review due cards from Set 7, then finish Set 8. Associate each small detail with its scene.
3. **TV & series, Set 9.** Work through the TV deck. Use the character groups below when unfamiliar names blur together.
4. **Deep cuts, Set 10.** Finish the final deck and try a Simulation in your weakest deck. Aim for at least 10/12 as a practice target, not a guarantee of event performance.

Before the event, spend ten minutes on due cards and your weakest topic. Say an answer aloud before looking at the choices to check recall rather than recognition. A Simulation draws from the selected deck only. The category filter controls the answer wall, not the quiz.

Wrong answers become due after ten minutes. Correct answers return after longer intervals. You can always use Full deck or Simulation if Adaptive 12 says a deck is cooling down.

## Five traps worth learning cold

1. Vader says **"No, I am your father."** The famous "Luke" version is a misquote.
2. A **parsec measures distance**, not time. Han boasts about less than twelve parsecs.
3. **Luke destroys the first Death Star.** At the second, Wedge and Lando attack the reactor while Han's team fights on Endor.
4. **Cell 2187, block AA-23, docking bay 94.** The first two belong to Leia's prison; the third is the Falcon's berth in Mos Eisley.
5. **Grogu is not young Yoda.** The official Databank still lists Yoda's species as unknown.

Sources: [official trivia archive](https://www.starwars.com/news/star-wars-trivia), [Trivia Tuesday answers](https://www.starwars.com/news/star-wars-trivia-tuesday-answers), [Falcon](https://www.starwars.com/databank/millennium-falcon), [Yoda](https://www.starwars.com/databank/yoda), [Grogu](https://www.starwars.com/databank/grogu).

## Canon and Legends

The older Expanded Universe largely received the **Legends** label in 2014. Do not apply its family trees or events automatically to the current films and series. Thrawn first appeared in Timothy Zahn's *Heir to the Empire*, a Legends novel, and later entered current screen canon through Rebels. A shared character does not make both storylines identical.

Sources: [Lucasfilm's continuity announcement](https://www.starwars.com/news/the-legendary-star-wars-expanded-universe-turns-a-new-page), [Timothy Zahn on Thrawn](https://www.starwars.com/news/from-the-pages-of-star-wars-insider-timothy-zahn-talks-30-years-of-thrawn).
